About

A software company in Kathmandu, working anywhere.

Yama Technologies started in 2025 as a few engineers and quants who wanted to do serious technical work in Nepal without leaving Nepal. The company is how we made that possible.

Today the studio ships web, mobile and data software for clients in Nepal, Europe, the US and Australia; the finance desk trades on the NEPSE; and the lab keeps us honest. Same people, three doors.

Principles

  • Ship, then talk

    Demos over decks. Every project has something running by week two.

  • Senior by default

    Small teams of people who have owned production systems. No bench of juniors billed as seniors.

  • Open where we can

    Data, tooling and write-ups are public unless a client needs otherwise.

  • Curious on purpose

    We keep a lab because the best engineers we know need somewhere to ask hard questions.

FAQ

Working with a software company in Nepal.

Do you build software for clients outside Nepal?+

Yes. Most of our engineering team is in Kathmandu and we work with clients in Nepal, Europe, the US and Australia, overlapping working hours as needed.

What does software development cost in Nepal?+

Typically a third to a half of European or US rates for a senior team. We quote a fixed price for scoped projects or a monthly rate for ongoing product work, always in writing before we start.

Which technologies do you work with?+

React, TypeScript and Node for web; React Native and Flutter for mobile; Go, Rust and Python for services; Postgres, DuckDB and dbt for data; PyTorch and modern LLM tooling for AI. We choose boring, well-supported tools unless a problem demands otherwise.

How does a project start?+

A short call, a written scope with price and timeline within a week, and a running demo within two weeks of kick-off. You own all code and infrastructure from day one.

Why does a software company run a research lab?+

Because the people who build well tend to be the people who ask questions nobody has answered yet. The lab gives them room to do that, and Nepal has more than enough of those questions.
